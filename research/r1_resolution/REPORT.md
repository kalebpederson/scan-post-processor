# R1: Output resolution (ppi) vs legibility, and scanner JPEG quality

> Figures referenced below are not published (they show private scans).

All code/outputs in `research/r1_resolution/` (scripts: lib.py, prep_epson.py, ref_ocr.py, consensus.py, epson_eval.py, corpus_eval.py, corpus_ext.py, jpeg_eval.py, panels.py, crop_ssim.py, make_tables.py; raw results `*_results*.json`; tables regenerated by make_tables.py into work/tables.md).

## Recommendation (summary)
- **Small printed text (6-7 pt, incl. light gray halftone): 200 ppi** is the knee. Recall climbs steeply up to ~150-200 ppi and is flat within CI beyond that (halftone class 0.87 @150, 0.91 @200, 0.92 @250, 0.91 @300). 150 ppi is the minimum acceptable (about -4 points vs 200 on the hardest text, CIs overlap); below 133 ppi small text degrades visibly and measurably. For normal worksheet text (10-12 pt) 100-150 ppi is already at the OCR plateau (corpus median 0.97-0.98).
- **Handwriting (pen/pencil, exponents, fractions): 150 ppi** is enough. Strokes are thick and large; in the panels, 150 ppi is not distinguishable from the 300/600 source at 100% and 200% view, and 100-120 ppi is only slightly soft at 200% zoom. Light pencil is the most sensitive (SSIM table); 150 still fine.
- **Single default: 200 ppi** (small text safe, handwriting overkill but cheap). If file size dominates and the content is regular worksheets, 150 ppi is defensible. Do not go below 133 for anything with tiny text.
- **Scan at 300 dpi and downsample: fine.** Real hardware scans at 150/200/300/400 match simulated downsampling from 600 within noise, and 300-dpi hardware downsampled to 150/200 is as good as or better than a direct low-dpi scan (E3/E4).
- **JPEG q75 from simple-scan does not measurably hurt** at 300 dpi input once downsampled to <=200 ppi (J1; all paired diffs within noise, ~46 dB PSNR at 150 ppi). q85 is a harmless safety margin; higher qualities and 4:4:4 give no measurable legibility gain.

## Method and tools
- tesseract 5.5.0 (leptonica 1.86.0, libjpeg-turbo 2.1.5), `-l eng --oem 1 --psm N --dpi D tsv`, word-level rows, gray 8-bit input, conf as reported. Epson: `--psm 11` (sparse text; yielded the most words, and psm 3 repeated as a robustness check in E2). Corpus: `--psm 3`. psm kept constant within each experiment. OpenCV 5.0.0, Pillow 12.3.0.
- Downsample: `cv2.INTER_AREA` from gray source to r ppi; viewer model: `cv2.INTER_LINEAR` back up to the 600-dpi grid (Epson; 1200-source is also compared on the 600 grid, a stated deviation to keep OCR cost sane) or the 300-dpi grid (corpus). "direct" = OCR on the downsampled image with `--dpi r` (reference only).
- Epson page rotated 90 deg clockwise (confirmed with `tesseract --psm 0`: rotate 90, confidence 7.1; and re-OCR after rotation reported 0).
- Reference (Epson): words from OCR of the 600 and 1200 dpi scans agreeing in text (case-sensitive; punctuation-only tokens dropped, leading/trailing punctuation stripped) and in position (bbox centers within 20 px on the 600 grid) -> **388 reference words** (581 at 600, 616 at 1200 before intersecting). Classes by mean darkness (255-gray) of the 600-dpi bbox: bold >=50 (54 words), mid 35-50 (64), light gray halftone <35 (270). Caveat: "bold" contains many key-glyph tokens (shortcut symbols mis-OCRed into stable junk like "F8", "OFS") that are inherently unstable; the meaningful bold-heading subset is the 16 alphabetic bold words (Running, Debugging, Navigation, Editing, ...). n=16 is tiny.
- Metrics: (1) multiset word recall (spec); (2) positional recall: same text and center within 20 px, after fitting a per-image linear registration (hardware scans are separate scans: y-scale 0.998 and ~5 px offset, found necessary; without it hardware scans scored ~0.10 lower, a registration artifact) used for class splits and paired bootstraps; (3) CER over reference lines (words grouped into lines by geometry, hyp words falling in the line box). CER is noisy (e.g. 0.189 at 150 is a single line-assignment mishap) and is shown for completeness only.
- The reference comes from 600-dpi OCR, so the 600-source row is self-referential (r=600 is 1.0 by construction) and recall is relative to "what OCR can read at 600/1200", not ground truth.
- Corpus: 25 native-300-dpi pages (2544x3295); 22 with >=20 reference words used (1795 ref words; median word-box height 34 px at 300 dpi, i.e. mostly ~11 pt text, so these pages contain little small text). Reference = native OCR with conf>=90. r=300 is the identity (1.0 by construction). Extra low r (50-90) added to locate where normal text breaks.
- Bootstrap CIs: 2000 resamples over words (paired for differences).

## Timing
Downsample with INTER_AREA, single thread, median of 7: 600-dpi gray (7016x5096) -> 150: 10 ms; 300-dpi color page -> 150: 22 ms (-> 133: 30 ms); 1200-dpi gray (14031x10200) -> 150: 196 ms. Trivial.

## Corpus JPEG inspection (what simple-scan writes)
`magick identify -verbose` over all 66 corpus JPEGs: the 150-dpi pages (14, 1272 px wide) are all **quality 75, 4:2:0 (2x2,1x1,1x1)** - this is simple-scan's setting. The 200-dpi pages are mixed: q61-78 mostly 4:2:0, plus 5 at q75 with **4:4:4** (1x1). The 300-dpi pages are mixed: quality estimates of 100 (10 files), 84 (2), 61-65 (6), "1" (6 files, with ImageMagick's estimate failing; their quantization tables are custom/flat - probably an app-resampled or re-saved copy), and one 4:2:2 q95. So the user's PDFs are not uniformly simple-scan q75; many 300-dpi pages were saved at q100 or by other tools. The corpus JPEGs being already lossy is a caveat for step 4 (reference noise from compression is included in both reference and test).

## Interpretation

**Small text (Epson, E1).** Positional recall (all words): 0.72 @100, 0.79 @120, 0.77 @133, 0.81 @150, 0.83 @175, 0.86 @200, 0.88 @250/300, 0.905 @400 (95% CI half-width ~ +/-0.04, so neighbouring rows are not separable; the trend is). Light halftone class: 0.78, 0.84, 0.83, 0.87, 0.90, 0.91, 0.92, 0.91, 0.93. Rise from 100 to 200 is ~13 points; 200 to 400 adds ~2 points (inside noise). Multiset recall shows the same shape (0.81 @100, 0.88 @150, 0.90 @200, 0.93 @250+). Knee: ~150-200 ppi. The residual gap to 1.0 even at 400 is mainly the self-referential reference and bilinear re-blur, not real loss. Direct OCR (no upsample) collapses below ~133 ppi (0.40 @100, 0.70 @120) because tesseract needs a minimum glyph size; the viewer-model numbers are the more relevant ones for humans, but both agree the plateau starts ~150-200.
Headings/bold words (n=16): 0.81 @100/120, 0.94-1.0 from 133 up - big text survives low density; these need 133 at most.
Source dependence (E2, E5): 600 vs 1200 sources and psm 3 vs psm 11 give the same plateau within noise; psm 3 is uniformly lower (it drops sparse words) but shows the same knee (0.70 @100 -> 0.80 @200 -> 0.85 @300 -> 0.89 @400), i.e. psm 3 hints the benefit continues slightly beyond 200 for this very hard page. Treat "200" as the knee for 6-7 pt halftone text, with mild gains up to 300.

**Hardware vs simulation (E3, E4).** At 150/300/400 the real scan and the 600-dpi simulation agree (diff -0.005, +0.003, -0.003, CIs include 0); at 200 the real scan was 0.036 lower (CI just excludes 0; multiset recall differs by only 0.002, so mostly noise/tesseract variance). Simulation is trustworthy. Scan-300-then-downsample vs direct low-dpi scan: to 150 ppi +0.054 [+0.021,+0.088] in favour of downsampling; to 200 +0.005 [-0.026,+0.039] (tie). Downsampling from 300 hardware is as good as sampling from 400/600/1200 for target <=200 (E5: no consistent ordering across sources, all differences within noise). So scanning at 300 and downsampling is safe; the extra benefit of 600 is not detectable for r<=250. (For r=300 output obviously source must be >=300.)

**Corpus (C1).** Normal worksheet text is far less demanding: median recall 0.97-0.98 and mean 0.95-0.96 flat across 100-250 ppi; p10 ~0.89-0.92 (driven by a few pages with small printed fractions/labels, n small). Pages with recall <0.90 at r=150: 2 of 22. Break point for normal text appears below 100: median 0.94 @90, 0.94 @75, 0.89 @60, 0.79 @50 (p10 0.84 @75, 0.60 @60). Page SSIM median: 0.966 @100, 0.983 @150, 0.988 @200. The 1e-2 non-monotonicity (e.g. 133 lower than 120) and per-page jumps (single29 n=21: 0.52 @133 vs 1.0 @150) are tesseract instability on few words, not real trends. Per-page numbers are listed above. This metric is at ceiling and says little about 150 vs 300; the Epson small-text experiment is the discriminating one.

**JPEG quality (J1).** Over r=133/150/200/300, q75 4:2:0 vs lossless: -0.003, -0.018, +0.015, -0.021 positional recall; none significant at 95% except isolated +/-0.02-0.03 swings that change sign across r (q75 4:4:4 at r=200 +0.023 and q92 at r=300 -0.026 are the only CIs excluding 0, in opposite directions - consistent with chance among 24 comparisons). Gray PSNR vs lossless at the same output density: q75 = 48/46/45 dB at 133/150/200 ppi and 39 dB at 300 ppi (JPEG artifacts get averaged out by downsampling); q92 = 50-53, q100 = 59. File size of the 300-dpi scan: q75 0.82 MB, q85 1.10, q92 1.55 (4:4:4: 1.85), q100 4.6. No measurable OCR benefit from q85+, 4:4:4, or q100. So q75 is OK for this OCR/legibility criterion at <=200 ppi output. Caveats: (a) grayscale OCR can't see chroma damage; 4:2:0 chroma subsampling affects the edges of thin colored ink (red/green/blue pen) and light colored strokes - not tested with a quantitative color metric; check the 4:2:0 vs 4:4:4 effect visually on colored pen before concluding it is harmless; (b) a single scan/page (n=388 words, CI +/-0.03); (c) q75 at 300 output (no downsampling) shows 39 dB, the largest damage; downsampling recovers it, so if output stays at 300 ppi the benefit of a higher quality is larger.

## Caveats / where OCR and humans disagree
- OCR is a proxy: tesseract is hurt by blur in ways humans are not, and helped by dictionary priors. In the panels, 100 and 120 ppi at 200% zoom look clearly soft for 6-pt gray text, yet remain readable; 150 and above look essentially like the source at 100% on both 110 and 160 ppi screens; difference between 200 and 300 is not discernible by eye. My visual reading agrees with the OCR knee at ~150-200, but this is my own inspection (one reviewer, an image reader), so please check the panels yourself.
- Halftone: the 150-dpi downsample of dot-pattern text can produce moire/blotching if not area averaged; INTER_AREA avoided visible moire in the panels. If the post-processor ever uses a non-area filter (bilinear/nearest on large factors) results will be worse than measured here.
- Handwriting recall was not measured by OCR (tesseract cannot read this handwriting reliably); only SSIM on crops and the visual panels support the 150 ppi claim.
- Reference words come from OCR at 600/1200 so words OCR cannot read even at high res are excluded; true illegible-at-low-r cases for such words are not counted.
- Single physical document for the hardware comparison; one scanner (Epson). Viewing panels use area downscale for shrink and bilinear for magnification (deviation: spec said bilinear; bilinear on a 0.37x shrink aliases and no real viewer does that).
- Corpus references: pages with q100/"q1" JPEGs vs q61-84 differ in noise; recall at 300 ppi pages is mostly big text.
